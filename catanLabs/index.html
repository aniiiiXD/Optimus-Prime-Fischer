Trade search

Allowing the fine-tuned network to search legal trade offers won 164/192 (85.4%) against three evolved upstream heuristics across two board sets, versus 146/192 for shipped search on the same boards. The paired gain was +9.4 points, 95% board-bootstrap interval +2.6 to +16.2. On a separate Heuristic-v1 holdout it won 59/64 versus 53/64, a smaller uncertain gain. Its 128-game confirmation took 458 seconds versus 245 seconds for shipped search. This is a slower upstream-engine specialist with perfect-information search.

CPU training worked

A Rust environment and masked PPO added 9.51 million decisions in about 482 CPU seconds. The new raw policy gained 18.8 points against Heuristic-v1 and 22.7 against Heuristic-v2 on matched seeds; both paired intervals excluded zero.

Search needed the right actions

The original AlphaBot filters trade proposals before it searches. The stronger raw policy did not improve this filtered search: 148 versus 153 wins against Heuristic-v1, and 87 versus 88 against Heuristic-v2. Enabling legal trade proposals in search produced the stronger evolved-bot result above.

Catanatron transfer check

We mapped the fine-tuned PPO policy into the fair Catanatron 10-point arena and masked it to visible information and legal moves. On 16 matched boards with four seats, it won 8/64 against Value and 8/64 against AlphaBeta1; the current fair-value agent won 21/64 and 19/64. No illegal actions occurred. The upstream search result did not carry over as a strong standalone policy. Read the transfer report.

What the model learned

It learned a player-relative observation and legal-action scoring policy from 4,000 unique positions across 14 games. Validation action agreement was 41.6% on three whole held-out games.

What it did not prove

Imitating a shallow search bot did not reproduce its playing strength. The best held-out-loss checkpoint was epoch 8; later epochs overfit. This checkpoint is not promoted.

How the learning loop will work

Start with examples that teach a legal, sensible policy. Use reinforcement learning only after that baseline can finish games reliably.

Play

Engine and mixed bots

Generate games with varied seats, seeds, and strategies.

Record

Unique observations

Keep player-visible state, phase, legal actions, and outcome.

Label

Teacher decisions

Cache value or search choices, plus action scores when affordable.

Compare

MLP versus Transformer

Same data, split, training budget, and parameter range.

Improve

PPO and opponent league

Fine-tune the best supervised policy against frozen styles.

Promote

Locked tournament

Keep a model only if it wins more on held-out games.

The fair CPU strategy is now the teacher and playing-strength target for the next model. A Transformer must match or exceed it in games before promotion.

A good dataset needs more than different boards

Like the Connect Four work, we will seek unique positions and tactical patterns. In Catan, a position also depends on the phase, resources, player perspective, and public opponent state.

First pipeline pilot

4,000

unique decision observations collected and deduplicated across 14 completed games.

  • Setup and growthProduction balance, first settlement, road paths, expansion races.
  • EconomyScarce resources, bank ratios, ports, hand limits, build timing.
  • Dice and robberProduction odds, discards, blocking, and Knight decisions.
  • Victory raceCity upgrades, Longest Road, Largest Army, hidden-point uncertainty.
  • Trading, if requiredOffer value, accept/reject/counter, and opponent-specific responses.

Split train, validation, and test data by whole games and board seeds. Do not let near-identical positions leak across the split.

What public work suggests

The useful lesson is not “Transformers always win.” Different Catan projects use different rules and solve different subproblems, so we need same-rules comparisons of our own.

Four-player trading

A small specialist can learn negotiation

A 2015 deep-RL study trained a roughly 14k-parameter MLP for trade offers and replies. It reported 53.36% against three heuristic traders over 10,000 test games. The rest of play stayed with JSettlers.

Board-aware networks have precedent

Cross-dimensional neural networks combine board and player features, but that study is two-player and excludes player trading. Deep Catan explores CNNs with Expert Iteration and search.

What must be fixed before final claims

These are the current limits of our exam. Addressing them makes later model scores more trustworthy.

Hidden-information boundary

The selected policy passed a hidden-state perturbation check on over 100 decisions. The harness still passes a full game object, so a restricted observation interface remains the stronger guarantee.

Player-to-player offers

The current action protocol omits negotiation. Add it if the target Catan rules expect resource offers between players.

Rules and opponent mix

Two mixed-bot compositions are now tested. The intended competition rules still need to be matched before treating these scores as transferable.

The next four moves

The stronger CPU strategy gives the next model a concrete target: win at least as often under the corrected protocol.

  1. Keep the incumbentThe fair strategy and its parameters stay fixed for future comparisons.
  2. Collect stronger examplesGenerate varied states from the selected policy and mixed opponents.
  3. Compare compact modelsGive an MLP and Transformer the same data and CPU budget.
  4. Test new seedsPromote a trained model only after it matches the incumbent in playing strength.
